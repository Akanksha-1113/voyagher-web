Core Safety Principles
Rule 1: Trust Your Instincts
If a situation feels wrong, leave immediately.
Indicators:
Someone is pressuring you.
You feel isolated.
A stranger insists on helping despite refusal.
A deal seems too good to be true.
Recommended action:
Move to a populated area.
Enter a hotel, store, or café.
Contact a trusted person.

Rule 2: Don't Reveal Too Much Information
Avoid telling strangers:
Hotel room number
Travel plans
Whether you are traveling alone
Financial information
Safer responses:
"My friends are meeting me later."
"My group is waiting for me."

Rule 3: Keep Multiple Payment Methods
Carry:
Main credit card
Backup card
Small amount of local cash
Emergency reserve
Never store all money in one place.

Common Tourist Scams
The Closed Attraction Scam
Claim:
 "Temple/museum is closed today."
Reality:
 Tourist is redirected elsewhere.
Common locations:
Major tourist attractions
Response:
 Verify independently.

The Friendly Stranger Scam
Scenario:
Stranger becomes very friendly
Offers tours
Requests money later
Response:
 Politely decline.

The Gem Scam
Claim:
 "Government promotion"
 "Special export deal"
Reality:
 Overpriced or worthless products.
Response:
 Avoid investment purchases while traveling.

Currency Exchange Scam
Warning signs:
Unclear rates
Refusal to provide receipt
Fast counting techniques
Response:
 Use reputable exchanges.

Fake Police Scam
Scenario:
 Someone claiming to be police asks:
Passport
Wallet
Cash inspection
Response:
 Request official identification.
 Ask to visit nearest police station.

Motorbike Damage Scam
Common in:
Tourist islands
Beach destinations
Scenario:
 Rental company claims damage.
Protection:
Photograph vehicle before use
Record video walkaround

Bar Tab Scam
Scenario:
 Unexpected charges appear.
Protection:
Review menu prices beforehand
Keep receipts
Check bill carefully
